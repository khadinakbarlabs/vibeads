# Example: a 30-day SaaS paid-media test

**Synthetic planning example.** This illustrates VibeAds output; it is not a recorded Actor result, native skill run or real campaign. The fictional product and all economics below are scenario inputs, not researched facts.

## Decision

Test intent-led Google search alongside a small Meta creative experiment. Keep a reserve unspent. Verify the conversion path before launch and use the first review to decide whether the assumptions warrant further spend.

## Brief and evidence

- Fictional offer: team task-management SaaS; audience: small agency teams; market: United States; language: English.
- Primary conversion: new paying customer, with first-order contribution supplied as USD 120/customer.
- Media budget: USD 1,500 over 30 days; target CPA assumption: USD 80/customer.
- Google keyword themes: project management software, team task management. These are scenario phrases; volume and CPC are **unknown**.
- No live competitor-ad records, keyword metrics, page screenshot or verified conversion events are available. Competitor messaging and creative ideas remain **hypotheses**.

## Budget

| Channel | Allocation | Active daily amount | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Google | USD 900 | USD 30/day | Test high-intent search phrases |
| Meta | USD 450 | USD 15/day | Test one original creative hypothesis |
| Unspent reserve | USD 150 | None | Await a separate allocation decision |

USD 900 + USD 450 + USD 150 = USD 1,500. Active planned spend is USD 1,350: USD 45/day × 30 days. Research, production and software charges are separate and are not approved by this media plan.

At the USD 80 CPA assumption, active spend models 16.875 customers. With USD 120 first-order contribution/customer, this models USD 2,025 contribution before ads and USD 675 after active ad spend, before omitted costs. This is conditional arithmetic, not a customer or profitability forecast. If the reserve were separately allocated, the full USD 1,500 would model 18.75 customers at the same assumed CPA.

First-order break-even CPA is USD 120 under the supplied contribution definition. In a separate Google scenario, USD 900 / assumed USD 3 CPC = 300 clicks. At an assumed 4% conversion rate, that models 12 customers and USD 75 CPA. At 2%, it models 6 customers and USD 150 CPA, above the stated first-order economic boundary. A real test must resolve the rate assumption.

## Creative and page brief

**Hypothesis:** agency owners care about reducing task handoff confusion. Original concept: demonstrate a real handoff in the product, then show the next owner and deadline. Draft hook direction: “Give every handoff a clear owner.” CTA: inspect the product workflow. No claim about time savings is made without product evidence.

Production needs an approved product capture, one concrete scenario and two hook variations. Do not copy competitor creative, invent a testimonial or generate media through this plugin. The landing page should show the same workflow and next step; its visible copy, mobile layout, signup/payment and consent behavior all need separate checks.

## Measurement and review

The advertiser owns launch and testing. Verify the paying-customer event, currency/value, deduplication, consent and aggregate analytics/billing reconciliation. State the actual attribution window, timezone and conversion lag before interpreting results.

Proposed first checkpoint: day 7, or USD 150 spent in a channel, whichever comes first. This is an initial operator-review heuristic, not a significance threshold or automatic pause. Allow the stated conversion lag before judging zero conversions. Review spend, qualified conversions, sales follow-through and page/measurement issues before allocating more.

Each channel must stay within its allocation through advertiser-configured controls and manual oversight. Pause and investigate delivery outside the approved budget or broken conversion measurement. Keep the reserve unspent until the advertiser approves its destination. No account change, purchase or schedule is executed by this document.
