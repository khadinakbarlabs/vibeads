# Budgeted media plan

Read [workflow rules](../ads/references/workflow-rules.md), [planning math](../ads/references/planning-math.md) and [media-plan template](../ads/references/templates/media-plan.md).

1. Establish objective, primary conversion, customer/offer, geography, launch dates, currency, available budget, contribution margin and sales/fulfillment capacity. If optional data is missing, state assumptions and proceed in planning mode. Never require API credentials for a plan.
2. Assess demand capture versus demand creation, expected creative capacity, sales cycle and measurement readiness. Pick the few channels the available budget can test. Do not spread a small budget across every supported research platform.
3. If the account, pixel or conversion event is new or unknown, use a staged learning plan with explicit creative hypotheses and measurement verification. Do not apply mature-account benchmarks or fixed event counts to missing history.
4. Derive the economic boundary from contribution profit, not revenue alone. Keep first-order economics distinct from validated lifetime economics; do not invent LTV, margin or a safe acquisition cost. Show inputs, formulas and low/base/high scenarios.
5. Allocate explicit currency amounts to channel/experiment and reserve. Totals must equal the approved media budget; daily amounts must reconcile with the stated number of days. Reserve remains unspent unless the advertiser separately allocates it. Research, production and software costs are separate lines, never hidden inside the media total.
6. Define campaign structure as an advisory proposal: intent/theme, audience hypothesis, offer, creative slate, landing-page path and primary conversion. Refer to current official platform requirements when selecting final bid strategies or formats; avoid unsupported claims about algorithm learning or targeting capabilities.
7. Write a phased plan: measurement/creative prerequisites, bounded test, review and optional expansion. Each test has an owner, budget, window, success criterion, minimum evidence, conversion-lag allowance and stop/rollback rule. Label heuristics as starting hypotheses to recalibrate.
8. Check arithmetic with an available host calculator or other permitted deterministic tool. Show the calculation trail and units. If no calculator is available, state that arithmetic was checked manually; do not imply an automated calculation ran.

Return a plan the advertiser can approve, with uncertainties and dependencies. A modeled 18.75 acquisitions at a USD 80 CPA from USD 1,500 is conditional arithmetic, not a promise of 19 customers. No spend is authorized or executed by producing the plan.
