# Original creative briefs

Read [workflow rules](../ads/references/workflow-rules.md) and the [creative brief template](../ads/references/templates/creative-brief.md).

1. Collect the offer, audience, awareness stage, platform/placement, desired action, approved product claims, tone and production capacity. Use research expressly supplied for this task. A competitor's claim does not prove the advertiser's product claim.
2. Separate customer/product evidence, the advertiser's actual performance data and public competitor patterns. Label each idea with its evidence IDs and why it may matter. If proof is missing, deliver a hypothesis and a proof requirement rather than inventing a statistic or testimonial.
3. Create three meaningfully different concepts: a customer problem, a demonstrable benefit and an objection/proof angle where evidence permits. Vary the idea rather than only synonyms. Keep brand claims truthful, avoid fabricated social proof, artificial urgency and unsupported superlatives.
4. For each concept, provide the hook, copy direction, CTA, visual/production brief, landing-page promise, source or hypothesis, audience and one measurable test. Produce text and production directions only; do not invoke image/video/audio generators or copy competitor assets.
5. Verify current platform specs and applicable policies from official documentation if final upload-ready copy is requested. Distinguish platform limits from display recommendations. If verification is unavailable, label specs unverified and deliver editable drafts without an upload-ready claim. Do not freeze changing specs or learning-phase requirements as universal rules.
6. Design one-variable tests with the measurement skill. Name the variant, control, primary conversion, time/exposure requirements, budget, owner and decision rule. An ad observed for 90 days is not proof it converts.

Return a compact approval slate and the recommended first concept with its evidence. Approval of a brief is not authorization to publish it or spend media budget.
